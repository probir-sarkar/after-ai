# After AI — daily changes

One dated line per day: what changed on the site, which page, and which source forced
the change. The daily update job reads this first so it does not repeat yesterday's work,
and so a claim that has gone stale is visible rather than buried.

Rules the daily job works to: every number needs two independent sources (primary first),
forecasts stay labelled as forecasts, editorial judgements stay labelled as judgements,
and no change is better than an invented one. One page per day, small diffs.

- **2026-10-27** — site-wide: footer now links Little Thinker, the blog and browserstay.com/projects,
  so the four projects cross-reference each other. No claims or figures touched.

- **2026-10-28** — `customer-service-rep`: added four metrics and replaced the claims list with the
  September 2026 Canaries figures (most-exposed occupations −0.5% year on year, least-exposed +1.0%;
  workers 22–25 in exposed jobs −3.7%, a 36th consecutive month of decline, now reaching 26–30 at
  −2.8%) and the Banque de France hiring data (permanent-contract hires for 21–29-year-olds in the
  most exposed fifth ~28% below November 2022, against 8%). The 19% level gap and the 3.7% annual
  rate are now explicitly separated in the prose so they are not read as one figure. New sources:
  `adp-canaries-sep26`, `bdf-463`. Also fixed `scripts/seo.mjs`, which still asserted the old
  after-ai.probir.dev host in robots.txt and had failed every build since the domain move.

- **2026-10-28** — site-wide: every job page and section page now has its own OG card (name + verdict +
  domain), generated by `scripts/og.mjs` at deploy time. The single shared `/og.png` told a reader nothing
  about the page they were opening. No claims or figures touched.

- **2026-10-31** — `software-developer`: replaced the vague entry-rung point with the Statistics Canada
  hiring-flow study published 28 October 2026 (Labour Force Survey, 2006–2026): hiring rate for
  15–29-year-olds in coding-intensive occupations fell 2.5% → 1.9% between the six months to
  November 2022 and the six months to August 2026, separations 2.4% → 3.5%. The same study's
  counter-evidence is now on the page rather than hidden — hires fell furthest where AI exposure is
  lowest (4.0% → 3.3%), coding hiring started falling early in 2022 before ChatGPT, and separation
  rates were flat across exposure groups, so the paper does not attribute the decline to AI. Added
  four metrics and the US posting shape from Indeed Hiring Lab (4.5% entry-level / 69.3% senior in
  Q1 2026, against 46% / ~14% nationally). Also removed literal `*democratised*` asterisks that were
  rendering in the page's threat list, and moved the horizon to 2022–2026. New sources:
  `statcan-hiring-2026`, `indeed-seniority`.

- **2026-11-04** — `graphic-designer`: new profile, the clearest case on the site of one role splitting
  in two. BLS (Occupational Outlook Handbook, last modified 29 October 2026) projects graphic designer
  employment down 2% from 253,100 jobs between 2025 and 2035 — 4,200 jobs — with all ~16,000 annual
  openings coming from replacement rather than growth, and names automated design tools such as AI as a
  productivity factor that reduces the need for these workers. Against that, the adjacent roles grow:
  art directors +4% (+5,900, $114,850 median), web developers and digital designers +5% (interface
  designers $104,000). The entry rung is where the damage is: UK entry-level graphic design hiring fell
  28% in the year to April 2026 (DSIT with LinkedIn, second-steepest of 38 occupations behind
  accountants at −29%), though the authors say the roles are hiring less at every seniority level and
  that this is not yet causal evidence of AI. Freelance image-creation posts fell 17% within eight
  months of image generators (Demirci, Hannane and Zhu, Management Science). The page carries the
  counter-case as prominently: Canva's November 2026 restructure affected 1,271 roles, 22% of staff,
  and its chief executive's note cites slowing growth and collapsed layers — it does not mention AI.
  New sources: `bls-graphic-designers`, `bls-art-directors`, `bls-web-designers`,
  `dsit-entry-level-uk`, `ms-freelance-image`, `canva-note`, `guardian-canva`. Dropped: the −18.49%
  graphic-design figure that aggregator posts attribute to the Management Science paper — the paper's
  own abstract states 17% for image creation, and the decimal figure appears to belong to a different
  paper's appendix, so only the verified number went on the site.

- **2026-11-07** — `junior-analyst`: the page's thesis ("the entry level is being redesigned, not
  deleted") now rests on observed payrolls rather than employer expectations alone. The October 2026
  Employment Situation (BLS, 6 November) added 165,000 jobs against a prior twelve-month average of
  48,000, with professional and business services +70,000 against a 15,000 average and professional,
  scientific and technical services +33,000 in the month — 269,000 added since the sector's October
  2025 low, and August/September revised up by 84,000. Four metrics replaced three: the +33,000 month,
  insurance payrolls −89,700 over the year, PwC's +35% for seniorised junior roles, and the 26% of
  AI-using hiring managers in Northeastern's survey who report hiring fewer entry-level candidates.
  The prose now puts the AI losses where the counts put them — insurance −3.0%, publishing −2.6%,
  motion pictures −5.6% over the year — and carries the counter-case: payroll data counts jobs not
  causes, the household survey weakened in the same month (involuntary part-time +339,000, U-6 7.9%),
  and both of the strongest numbers are forward-looking intentions. Horizon moved to 2019–2026. New
  sources: `bls-employment-oct26`, `cepr-oct26`, `northeastern-hiring-2026`.
