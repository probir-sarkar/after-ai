This is the most speculative profile on the site, and the only one with no claim attached — because
no public data describes a market that does not exist. What exists is the question. Agents are
starting to take real actions in the world: booking the appointment, sending the payment link,
confirming the order <Cite ids="mckinsey-agents" />. Every deployment currently answers "who pays
when the agent is wrong?" informally — a term of service, a human in the loop, a hope.

Industries have a way of answering that question formally. Insurance exists because unfamiliar risk
eventually gets priced. An underwriter for agent actions would read an operator's logs, guardrails
and rollback design the way a car insurer reads a driving record, and put a number on the residual
risk of delegation.

Why include something this speculative? Because the four tests read unusually well: total
accountability, a relationship with the insured, an indivisible seat. If delegation keeps growing,
the scarcity is not technical — it is people willing to stake a professional judgement on a
machine's behaviour. That scarcity is the job.
