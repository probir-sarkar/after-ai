An assistant’s day is a run of small tasks with hard edges: move the meeting, book the flight,
fix the header on page four, chase the signature, remember that the second version is the one the
client saw. Nearly all of it is text, calendar and contact, which is why this job sits on the
exposed side of the ledger — no body in a changing place, and a role that can be picked apart task
by task. Two of the four tests still hold. Someone is answerable when the travel goes wrong, and a
good assistant carries a relationship the org chart does not record.

### The group young workers left

The most precise evidence on clerical work in 2026 is not a survey of assistants. It comes from
the Current Population Survey, with occupations ranked by how much of their work each generation
of AI could do — chatbots, then reasoning models, then agents. Between the first half of 2023 and
the first half of 2026, occupations whose exposure score rose most lost about 8% of their workers
under 30 for every standard deviation of that rise. Their total employment and their unemployment
rate did not move.<Cite ids="dominski-labour-2026" />

That combination is the finding. If AI were replacing people, the counts would fall and
unemployment would rise. What happened instead is that the same number of jobs was held by a
different set of people: workers under 30 without degrees out, workers aged 30 to 49 with degrees
in, full-time.

The distribution of that loss is where assistants come in. Office and administrative support
accounts for 42% of the under-30 decline while employing 13% of young workers — the largest
contribution of any group, far ahead of its size. Among the individual occupations driving it,
customer service representatives carry 35% of the loss in the most exposed quartile, and
secretaries and administrative assistants, human resources workers, management analysts and
bookkeeping clerks follow at 5 to 8% each.<Cite ids="dominski-labour-2026" /> The group’s exposure
score rose 41 points across the period, close to business, legal and engineering work, in a group
that employs 11% of American workers.

### The door, not the floor

A second study, using the same survey to follow individuals month to month, asks what happened to
the people on the other side of that. Since generative AI spread, unemployment for workers aged 23
to 54 rose about a percentage point more in the most exposed occupations than in the least. The
cause is not separations: the rate at which people leave jobs in exposed occupations is unchanged.
It is job-finding. The monthly rate at which unemployed workers in the most exposed occupations
find work fell about six points more than in the least exposed, against a national average near
34%, which stretches the implied average spell of unemployment from about ten and a half weeks to
more than thirteen.<Cite ids="clevelandfed-jobfinding" />

The age split is the sharpest part of it. Between the least and most exposed occupations the
predicted unemployment gap is about two points for workers aged 23 to 30, and at most one point
for workers aged 45 to 54. The authors read the pattern as consistent with what some observers
call a “no hire, no fire” market: firms waiting to see what AI does to their workflows, and
shrinking their exposure to labour costs through attrition and slower hiring rather than
dismissal.

Both studies draw on the same monthly survey, and neither claims AI caused the change — an
exposure score measures what a model can do, not what a firm did with it.

### The reading

Two jobs wear this title. One is calendar logistics and document formatting, and it is being
absorbed. The other is judgement held in trust: knowing that Thursday’s meeting is the one that
matters, that the number in the draft is wrong, that the client needs to hear it from a person
before it reaches the board. That half is not exposed, and it is not where anyone starts.

The apprenticeship ran through the first half. An assistant learned an executive’s priorities by
managing the calendar, and learned what a good document looks like by fixing bad ones. That route
is now the part that is closing, which leaves the durable half of the job reachable mostly by
people already inside it.
