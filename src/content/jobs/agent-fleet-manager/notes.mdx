Nobody is hired under this title today, and we are not predicting that they will be next year. What
exists is the pattern. Coding tools now ship <Cite ids="mckinsey-agents" /> a coordinator that plans
work, delegates it to as many subagents as needed, and brings the finished work back for review —
months-long projects run as a fleet, not a conversation. The pattern is the job description waiting
for a name.

The human version of that role would not write code. It would own the goal, the budget, the review
bar and the decision about what a fleet is allowed to touch — the parts of management that are
about accountability rather than task execution. That is why the profile reads high on the bundle
test and low on body: the seat is indivisible even if every task inside it is delegable.

Two endings are possible, and honesty requires naming both. The role could arrive as a title — the
way "forward-deployed engineer" did, which went from obscure to thousands of postings in a year
<Cite ids="fde-data" />. Or the coordinator layer could absorb the management work the way it
absorbed delegation, leaving the job inside existing engineering management. The signal to watch is
hiring language: the first job postings that say "manage a fleet of agents" will be the moment this
stops being a possibility.
