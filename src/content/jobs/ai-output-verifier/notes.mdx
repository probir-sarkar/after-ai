Every agent deployment creates a standing obligation: someone must review outputs, handle
exceptions, assure quality and document what happened <Cite ids="mckinsey-agents" />. Today that
obligation is spread invisibly across existing jobs — the editor checking the draft, the analyst
checking the model's numbers, the support lead checking the bot's answers.

Microsoft's study of 200,000 real AI conversations found the same thing from the other side: in the
most AI-applicable occupations, the work that stays most human is exactly this — verification,
judgement, communication <Cite ids="ms-applicability" />. The possibility is that this distributed
task concentrates into a profession: domain experts paid specifically to certify machine output at
volume, the way quality assurance condensed out of manufacturing.

The threat is the interesting part. Verifier models — AI checking AI — are already being built, and
they will take the volume. What they cannot take is responsibility: a signed human judgement about
whether the system is fit to operate. So the durable version of this job is narrower than the name
suggests — less "check the output", more "own the decision to trust the checker".
